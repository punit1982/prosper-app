## D1 · The diagnosis that produced v5.1

**GROW v5.0 classified a company on one axis. PROSPER v11 and v16 classified on five.** The measurement basis (B1–B8) and the scale tier (S0–S4) existed, were operative, and were lost in the consolidation that produced GROW. The consequence was measurable: on a pre-revenue company roughly **34 of 100 points of the score were placeholders** — margin room forced to 5, economic engine near zero by construction, and two of the three Worth sub-tests unavailable.

**PROSPER v11 basis B6 already contained the fix:** *"D1's weight is set to 0 and transferred to D6. D1 is not scored and not printed."* No default. The weight moves to something observable. §7.2 restores it as a general rule.

## D2 · The four decisions, and the evidence behind each

**1 · Traction weight — cut where the book is soft, held where it is contractual.**
TRIAD v1.1 tested contracted backlog, remaining performance obligations, deferred revenue, incremental margin, cash runway, leverage, gross-margin quality, revenue growth rate and megatrend membership across **1,577 observations, 265 companies, 9 start years** — *"all null or backwards."* Against that, the loser panel of five companies that each lost 82–98% from a peak **failed on the moat question first in 5 of 5 cases**, each capped on that question alone before any valuation argument. Traction therefore keeps full weight only in P3 (design-in, where the book is a dated contractual design win) and P4 (contracted infrastructure, where the book *is* the asset), and is cut by 2–6 points elsewhere. The weight moved to moat.

**2 · The regime factor adjusts the multiple and never the score.**
Eleven backtested cases carried a completely correct megatrend and lost 78–97%. Solar scaled, telehealth became normal, remote work became permanent, and the equities were destroyed. **Membership predicts nothing about whether this company earns the profits.** What the regime does affect is what a buyer pays for a given stream — so it sits on the terminal multiple, capped at ±15%, inside the ±25% stress, with a hard ×1.00-and-flag rule where the theme has no row. *Five coverage gaps were found in three days; applying an adjacent row by default once moved a price by 13.6%, enough to flip fair into rich.*

**3 · Two verdicts, both quantified.**
Measured: a score containing price **fell ten points when AXTI rose 63%** on a blowout result, and the same on CRDO and PLTR. *"A number that punishes good news cannot answer 'should I stay invested.'"* Durability is therefore price-free and is a 0–100 score. Entry is arithmetic — expected return against the required return — and prints a four-level price ladder rather than a single word, which is what makes it a range. The 20% price ceiling was suspended because it had no calibration and because **14 of 15 winners never filled a limit 15% below spot**; a rule requiring a discount to spot systematically excludes the names the framework exists to find.

**4 · Tie-breaks — fact first, then conservatism.**
The two prior rules were answering different questions. Where the gross profit sits is an observation that identifies which business this is. The higher required return is a policy against flattering. Sequenced, both survive. **The direction of the policy matters:** it shipped stated backwards once, and correcting it moved one name's entry level from $479 to $419 — two sell rungs on a name trading at $895.

## D3 · What accuracy means, and why 80% is the wrong target

**The sleeve arithmetic.** Fifteen names, one winner at 10×, losers at −70%: the sleeve **loses 0.42%**. Two winners at 3.6× with losses capped at −40%: it works. **The requirement is two in fifteen — a 13% hit rate.**

**And chasing a higher hit rate destroys the return.** In the 173-name, ten-year study the highest-scoring quality quintile produced **11.4% five-baggers** against **25.7%** for the lowest. A screen optimised for being right most of the time selects against the outcomes that pay.

**Three levers, multiplicative, worth +17.8 percentage points together:**

| Lever | Gain | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Hit rate 1 → 2 in 15 | +5.14pp | Room-and-mechanism screen: 25.0% five-baggers against 7.7% |
| Loss size −70% → −40% | +2.23pp | Rule 18 — the one reason, checked quarterly |
| Winner 10× → 20× | **+5.30pp** | **No time stop, ever.** A 36-month stop destroyed 6 of 7 winners; one sat at 1.11× before returning 16.8× |

**What is committed at 80% or better** is stated in §8.9: blow-up avoidance ≥95%, interval coverage ≈80%, retrieval integrity 100%. **Directional accuracy is recorded without a threshold**, deliberately.
