# GROW v5.1 — ANNEX A · COMPANIES WITHOUT HISTORY
**Load when the proof stage is S0, S1 or S2, or the measurement basis is M6 or M7.**

> **Why this annex exists.** The core framework anchors on what a company has already earned: its own best full year, the peer median at its size, its five-year multiple range, its eight-quarter guidance record. **A company in the middle of a transformation has none of those.** Under v5.0 it therefore collected a placeholder score of 5 on roughly a third of its weight — and a placeholder 5 is a Hold-shaped number that carries no information. Five names ran that way in one week and every one came out Hold or Sell.
>
> **This annex replaces the missing anchors with forward evidence rather than filling them with a default.** Where a substitute exists it is scored and the weight stays. Where none exists the weight is removed and redistributed under §7.2. **Nothing is ever defaulted.**

**The five-year scale test, stated pass or fail in every S0–S2 memo, in these words:** *will this be a real business at the valuation date, or is this a venture bet?* A fail is not a refusal to rate — it is a statement that the Bull case carries the whole answer, and it caps Durability at 55.

# A2 · THE FORWARD VALUATION ENGINE

**A company without history cannot be valued by growing its current earnings forward. It is valued by establishing the end state and discounting back.** Four methods build the end state and one tests it. **At least two of the four must be built, and where they disagree by more than 2× the memo says so and takes the lower.**

## Method A · Top-down, TAM to SAM to SOM

```
End revenue = market size at the valuation date × serviceable share × realistic capture × price
```

**Rules that make this honest rather than decorative:**
- **The market size comes from a named third party with a date, never from the company's own deck.**
- **The serviceable share is the part this company can physically address** — the partners it has signed, the geographies it is licensed in, the capacity it will have built.
- **A capture rate above 15% in a market the company does not already lead is scored down and the reason printed.** A capture rate above 30% requires a monopoly argument stated in one sentence.
- **The gap between the market forecast and the company's own claim is printed.**

## Method B · Bottom-up unit economics — the real underwrite

**Build from physical units, never from a percentage growth rate.**

```
End revenue = units in service × output per unit × price per unit
End gross profit = end revenue × target gross margin at scale
```

Worked shapes: *satellites × subscribers per satellite × monthly revenue per subscriber × 12* · *gigawatt-hours of factory capacity × revenue per kilowatt-hour × utilisation* · *ports × share × average selling price, plus attach* · *launches × price per launch, plus the systems line.*

**Where the unit margin is negative today, the target gross margin at scale must be evidenced by a named analogue operating at that scale — not asserted.** This is the single most load-bearing input in the whole annex and it is always Class C.

## Method C · The growth path is a curve, not a line

**Penetration follows an S-curve. Model it as one.** Innovation phase 0–5% of the addressable pool, early majority 5–50% — **where most of the return is** — maturity 50–90%.

```
Implied CAGR = (end revenue ÷ current revenue)^(1/n) − 1
```

**Print the implied CAGR and test it against history: a rate above 80% sustained for five years is close to unprecedented outside a monopoly, and a model requiring one says so in the memo.** State where on the curve the company sits today, and whether the modelled path is pre- or post-inflection.

## Method D · Analogue and replacement cost

**Two independent sanity checks, both printed.**
- **Analogue:** the closest real company at the same stage of the same kind of build, named, with what it was worth at the equivalent point.
- **Replacement cost:** what it would cost to rebuild the asset from nothing — *168 satellites at $25m is $4.2bn of replacement cost; if the enterprise value is $28bn, the market is paying $23bn for the spectrum, the network and the licences, and the memo says so in those words.*

## The reverse test — what the price already requires

**Run on every S0–S2 name, without exception.** It requires no forecast of ours and cannot be flattered.

```
Value at the valuation date implied by today's price = today's enterprise value × (1 + required return)^n
Revenue that implies                                 = that value ÷ exit multiple ÷ target margin
Growth that implies                                  = (that revenue ÷ current revenue)^(1/n) − 1
```

**Print the sentence:** *"At today's price the market is already paying for $X of revenue at the valuation date, which is Y% a year from here. Method B supports $Z."* **The gap between X and Z is the entire investment case, in one line.**

## Putting it together — expected value, not a point

```
Expected value = Σ (probability of each case × value in that case)
```

**Four cases, probabilities stated and summing to one, each with its own revenue, margin, multiple, share count and debt.** **The Break case is bankruptcy or recapitalisation, not a mild version of the base** — for a company that is not yet funded to its milestone, the Break case is a real branch with a real weight, and where its probability exceeds 40% Durability caps at 55.

**The probabilities are Class C and are named as such in the driving-inputs table.** Where more than two of the four probabilities are pure judgement with no base rate or named analogue behind them, **the Entry output is a range rather than a word.**

# A3 · THE VALUE-CHAIN LAYER TEST

**Where a company sits in its chain predicts its economics more reliably than what industry it is in.** Assign one and print it.

| Layer | Economics | What it means for the model |
|---|---|---|
| **Upstream · build and launch** | Capital-heavy, low margin, and historically a small share of total industry revenue | Target gross margin caps at the best any named peer has earned at scale. A model assuming software-like margins here is rejected |
| **Midstream · operate** | The transition zone — running the asset, logistics, integration | Target margin evidenced by a named operator, not by the industry's headline |
| **Downstream · data and services** | Build once, sell repeatedly. **The large majority of industry revenue accrues here** | Software-like margins are permitted where the company actually reaches this layer, and the memo states what has to happen for it to get there |

**Most companies in a new industry are modelled at the layer they hope to reach rather than the one they occupy. State both, and build the base case at the layer they occupy today.**

# A4 · FOLLOW THE MONEY — three questions, all answered in the memo

1. **Who funds the build?** Government procurement, customer prepayments, equity, debt — with the amount and the date. **A build with no identified funding source is a Break case, not a base case.**
2. **Who pays the revenue at the end state?** Named: the subscriber, the hyperscaler, the utility, the defence ministry. **"The market" is not a payer.**
3. **Who loses?** The incumbent whose business this displaces, named. **If nobody loses, the revenue is being assumed rather than taken, and the model is a forecast of a market rather than of a company.**

---

# A5 · THE PRE-INVESTMENT CHECKLIST — five questions, four must pass

Applied before the forward engine is trusted at all. **Fewer than four passes and the Entry verdict caps at HOLD regardless of arithmetic.**

1. **Is gross margin actually improving, quarter over quarter?**
2. **Is a real customer paying more this quarter than last?**
3. **Is there cash to reach scale without a raise below the last raise price?**
4. **Is the optionality free** — is the second business funded by the first, rather than by shareholders?
5. **Is the party who loses weaker than this company?**
